## What is the current regulatory position on crypto remittances in Pakistan?

Pakistan's regulatory position on crypto remittances is currently in a state of development, with a clear intent to establish a formal framework under the Pakistan Virtual Assets Regulatory Authority (PVARA). While the [State Bank](/blog/the-state-bank-of-pakistans-position-on-virtual-assets-explained/) of Pakistan (SBP) has historically maintained a cautious stance on virtual assets, the government is moving towards regulated acceptance to combat illicit finance and leverage technological advancements.

Historically, the State Bank of Pakistan (SBP) has expressed concerns regarding virtual assets due to risks associated with money laundering, terrorism financing, and consumer protection. The SBP's position on virtual assets has been explained in detail in previous analyses. However, the establishment of PVARA signals a shift towards a regulated environment rather than an outright ban. The Financial Action Task Force (FATF) has significantly influenced this direction, pushing member countries, including Pakistan, to implement robust regulatory frameworks for virtual assets. This includes addressing [FATF Recommendation 15](/blog/what-is-fatf-recommendation-15-and-why-does-it-shape-pakistans-rules/), which specifically targets virtual assets and VASPs. The Securities and Exchange Commission of Pakistan (SECP) also plays a role in corporate structuring and oversight for entities operating in this domain, complementing PVARA's specific [virtual asset](/blog/outsourcing-and-third-party-risk-for-virtual-asset-firms/) mandate.

The current regulatory landscape is characterised by:

*   **Ongoing Consultations:** PVARA is actively engaging with stakeholders to finalise the regulatory framework, including specific rules for virtual asset-based remittance services.
*   **Emphasis on AML/CFT:** A core objective is to prevent the use of virtual assets for illicit financial activities, aligning with international standards.
*   **Licensing Requirements:** Future operations in virtual asset remittances will almost certainly require a specific licence from PVARA, similar to other VASP activities.
*   **Phased Implementation:** The rollout of comprehensive regulations is expected to be phased, allowing the industry to adapt.

[Operators should](/blog/enforcement-powers-what-a-regulator-can-actually-do-to-a-vasp/) note that while the framework is under consultation, the general direction points towards strict oversight and mandatory licensing for any entity facilitating virtual asset transfers, including those for remittance purposes.

## Who is considered a Virtual Asset Service Provider (VASP) for remittance purposes?

A Virtual Asset Service Provider (VASP) for remittance purposes generally includes any entity that facilitates the transfer of virtual assets on behalf of a natural or legal person, particularly when these transfers are intended to move value across borders. PVARA's definition is expected to align with international standards, encompassing a broad range of activities.

Under the anticipated PVARA framework, entities engaging in activities such as virtual asset exchange, transfer, custody, or participation in financial services related to virtual assets will likely be classified as VASPs. For remittance purposes, this specifically targets businesses that:

*   **Exchange fiat currency for virtual assets** to be sent abroad, or vice-versa.
*   **Transfer virtual assets** from one address or wallet to another on behalf of a customer, where the purpose is to move funds internationally.
*   **Provide custody services** for virtual assets that are part of a remittance transaction.
*   **Facilitate payment processing** using virtual assets, where the ultimate goal is cross-border value transfer. More information on the regulatory perimeter for crypto payment processors is available on our blog.

Essentially, any business acting as an intermediary in converting, transferring, or holding virtual assets for the purpose of facilitating a cross-border payment will likely fall under the VASP definition. ## What are the key compliance challenges for crypto remittance operators?

Crypto remittance operators face significant compliance challenges primarily centred on Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Counter-Terrorism Financing (CFT) obligations, ensuring robust customer due diligence, and adhering to emerging international standards like the Travel Rule. These requirements demand sophisticated systems and processes.

The nature of virtual asset transactions, characterised by their speed, global reach, and pseudonymity, amplifies the need for stringent compliance. Key challenges include:

1.  **Customer Due Diligence (CDD) and Know Your Customer (KYC):** Operators must collect and verify identity information for senders and receivers. This includes understanding the source of funds and source of wealth, a critical distinction in crypto KYC for VASPs. Robust customer due diligence for crypto exchanges is a practical walkthrough for operators.
2.  **Transaction Monitoring:** Implementing systems to detect unusual or suspicious transaction patterns is vital. This requires setting rules and thresholds for crypto transaction monitoring.
3.  **The Travel Rule:** This FATF-mandated requirement obliges VASPs to share originator and beneficiary information for transactions above a certain threshold. Understanding what the Travel Rule is and how it applies to Pakistani VASPs is paramount.
4.  **Sanctions Screening:** Operators must screen all parties involved in transactions against national and international sanctions lists. Building a workable process for sanctions screening for virtual asset firms is a continuous effort.
5.  **Risk Assessment:** Developing a comprehensive VASP risk assessment methodology for AML is fundamental to identifying and mitigating potential illicit finance risks specific to virtual asset remittance services.
6.  **Record Keeping:** Maintaining accurate and accessible records of all transactions and customer information for specified periods is a critical VASP record keeping obligation.

Meeting these challenges requires significant investment in technology, training, and personnel, including a dedicated compliance officer or MLRO. Building a crypto compliance function from scratch in 90 days is a common goal for new entrants.

## How does PVARA intend to regulate crypto remittance activities?

PVARA intends to regulate crypto remittance activities through a comprehensive licensing framework, imposing strict AML/CFT obligations, and establishing robust operational and governance standards. The goal is to integrate virtual assets safely into the financial system while mitigating risks.

The regulatory approach by PVARA will likely mirror global best practices for Virtual Asset Service Providers (VASPs). This will involve:

*   **Licensing:** All entities providing virtual asset remittance services will need to apply for a specific VASP licence. The VASP licensing service will guide applicants through this process. This includes meeting stringent "fit and proper" tests for directors and senior management, as well as demonstrating adequate capital requirements for virtual asset firms.
*   **AML/CFT Compliance:** Operators will be required to implement robust Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing programmes, including detailed KYC/CDD procedures, transaction monitoring, and suspicious transaction reporting. Understanding what a Suspicious Transaction Report (STR) is and when a VASP must file one is crucial.
*   **Operational Requirements:** Regulations will cover areas such as cybersecurity requirements for licensed virtual asset firms, business continuity planning for VASPs, and measures for the segregation of client virtual assets through custody rules.
*   **Consumer Protection:** Rules will aim to protect consumers, including requirements for clear disclosures, complaints handling, and client redress mechanisms.
*   **Regulatory Reporting:** Licensed VASPs will be subject to regular reporting requirements to PVARA, including financial and compliance data, as outlined in the VASP regulatory reporting calendar.

PVARA's framework is designed to bring clarity and legitimacy to the virtual asset sector, ensuring that remittance services operate transparently and securely. Operators should regularly check [pvara.org](https://pvara.org) for the latest updates on the regulatory framework.

## What are the tax implications for crypto remittances in Pakistan?

The tax implications for crypto remittances in Pakistan are an evolving area, with the Federal Board of Revenue (FBR) working to clarify how virtual asset transactions, including remittances, are treated under existing tax laws. Operators must anticipate taxation on profits and potentially on services.

While specific legislation on the taxation of virtual assets is still developing, the FBR's general approach suggests that gains from virtual asset activities could be subject to income tax or capital gains tax. For remittance operators, this could mean:

*   **Income Tax on Fees/Commissions:** Any fees or commissions charged by the VASP for facilitating remittance services will likely be treated as business income and subject to corporate income tax.
*   **Withholding Tax:** The FBR may introduce withholding tax on certain crypto transactions in Pakistan, which could impact how remittances are processed and the net amount received by beneficiaries.
*   **Sales Tax/VAT:** The provision of virtual asset services, including remittance facilitation, could eventually fall under the purview of sales tax or value-added tax (VAT), as discussed in Pakistan's crypto services: VAT and sales tax implications.
*   **Reporting Obligations:** VASPs will likely have reporting obligations to the FBR regarding transactions facilitated, similar to traditional financial institutions. Individuals receiving crypto holdings may also need to consider declaring crypto holdings to the FBR.

It is important for operators to engage with tax professionals to understand their specific obligations as the FBR's stance solidifies. The distinction between income and capital gains for crypto is a key area of FBR focus. As regulations are finalised, Sarzif Policy will continue to provide regulatory updates on our blog.

## What are the risks of operating without a licence in this sector?

Operating a virtual asset remittance service in Pakistan without the required licence from PVARA carries severe legal, financial, and reputational risks. These include enforcement actions, substantial penalties, criminal prosecution, and the inability to operate legally within the formal financial system.

The Pakistani authorities, including PVARA, the SBP, and law enforcement agencies, are increasingly focused on regulating the virtual asset space. Operating outside the forthcoming regulatory perimeter can lead to:

*   **Enforcement Actions:** PVARA's enforcement powers include issuing warnings, imposing fines, restricting operations, and even revoking licences. For unlicensed entities, these actions could involve cease and desist orders.
*   **Financial Penalties:** Unlicensed operations can result in significant monetary fines, which can severely impact a business's viability.
*   **Criminal Charges:** Engaging in unlicensed financial services, particularly those involving cross-border transfers, can lead to criminal prosecution under anti-money laundering and other financial crime statutes. Pakistani courts have treated virtual assets in varying ways so far, but the trend is towards formal regulation.
*   **Asset Seizure:** Authorities may freeze or seize assets associated with unlicensed operations.
*   **Reputational Damage:** Being identified as an unlicensed or non-compliant operator can destroy public trust and make it impossible to attract legitimate customers or partners.
*   **Exclusion from Formal Finance:** Unlicensed entities will be unable to integrate with traditional banking systems, hindering their ability to scale or offer seamless services.

Operators must prioritise obtaining the necessary licences and adhering to all regulatory requirements to ensure legal and sustainable operations. Failure to do so exposes the business and its principals to unacceptable levels of risk.
